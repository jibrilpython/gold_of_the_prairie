# Gold of the Prairie

Description:

Gold of the Prairie is a mobile application for collectors of agricultural antiquities, historians of farming science, and preservers of rural heritage, dedicated to cataloging the manual grain inspectors' testing tools, mechanical triage sifts, and analog seed counters that determined the quality of wheat, corn, and barley before the era of digital moisture sensors and automated optical kernel sorters.
Systematize your collection of the guardians of the harvest—from polished brass "trier" probes used to sample deep inside grain sacks to mechanical hand-cranked seed docks, from balance-beam test weight scales with miniature brass buckets to vintage nested wooden sifting screens that separated chaff from the perfect kernel.
Create a digital archive of heavy industry by recording the instrument type, manufacturer, temperature range, era, and the specific steel mill, foundry, or ceramic kiln each specimen once calibrated.


## Bespoke Object Fields (Model "Harvest Instrument"):

•	Granary Registry Ledger: A unique, system-generated alphanumeric shipping and grading code (e.g., GOP-GRAIN-9942-WHEAT-M). 

•	Inspection Classification: The specific testing methodology utilized (e.g., Core Sampler, Triage Sieve, Test-Weight Scale, Seed Docking Mill, Kernel Count Board).

•	Artisan Hallmark: Fictionalized agricultural instrument works and scale foundries (e.g., PrairieCraft IronWorks, AetherGrain Scientific, Great Plains Balance Co).

•	Volumetric Capacity Bounds: The specific physical measurement limit or sample size the tool accommodates (e.g., 1 Pint Bushel, 30-inch Insertion Depth, 500-Kernel Matrix).

•	Sieve Mesh Geometry: For sifting tools, the precise layout and shape of the perforations (e.g., Triangular buckwheat holes, 1/14-inch round perforations, slotted flax screens).

•	Balance Beam Graduation: The specific historical measurement markings stamped onto the scaling arm (e.g., Pounds per Bushel, Kilograms per Hectoliter, raw grain ounces).

•	Millwork & Hardware Metallurgy: The primary construction materials of the sampling tool (e.g., Sheet brass jacket, polished copper hopper, oil-rubbed white oak frame).

•	Physical Proportions: The absolute physical length of the sampling probe or the spatial box dimensions of the sieve nest, along with the dry mass.

•	Preservation Soundness: The physical state of the hardware (e.g., Screen mesh tension, level-bubble vial integrity, wood seasoning cracks, brass tarnish level).

•	Harvest Ground Zero: The fictionalized regional agricultural landscape or historic grain elevator circuit where the item was actively deployed to grade crops (e.g., Red River Valley elevator route, historic midwest corn belt depot, forgotten coastal flour mill warehouse).
